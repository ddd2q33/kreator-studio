::: chapter number=1 title="Understanding Trauma Responses" intro="What happened to you was real. So is the way your body and mind learned to protect you." quote="Understanding what happened to your brain and body is the first step toward healing." :::

Trauma is not the event itself — it is what happens inside your nervous system when an experience is **too much, too fast, or too alone**. The mind may forget details; the body remembers.

This chapter helps you recognize the protective patterns you developed, not as flaws, but as adaptations that once kept you safe.

::: box insight title="Insight"
Trauma responses are survival adaptations, not personal failures. Hypervigilance, numbness, and avoidance were once the smartest available answers.
:::

## Common trauma responses

- **Hypervigilance** — a nervous system still scanning for danger
- **Emotional numbness** — protection through distance
- **Avoidance** — steering life away from anything that resembles the threat
- **Shame patterns** — the quiet belief that you are the problem

::: box science title="Science Behind It"
During threat, the **amygdala** activates the body's alarm system while the **prefrontal cortex** — the area responsible for reasoning and perspective — temporarily goes offline. This is why "just think positive" rarely works: the thinking brain is not in charge during a trauma response. With repeated experiences of safety, neuroplasticity allows these pathways to slowly reorganize.
:::

Take a breath before reading on. Notice what you feel in your chest, jaw, and shoulders as you meet these names for old experiences.

::: tool number=01 title="The Trauma Brain Map"
**Purpose:** Understand which part of your nervous system is driving in a difficult moment.

**Why This Matters:** Naming your state shifts activity back toward the thinking brain and restores a sense of choice.

**How To Use:**
1. Pause when you notice a strong reaction.
2. Ask yourself: *Am I in alarm, shutdown, or regulated right now?*
3. Write what you notice on the map below.

**Reflection Questions:**
1. What situations send me into alarm fastest?
2. What does "regulated" feel like in my body?

**Practice:**
Set one reminder today to check your state. One sentence is enough.

::: chapter number=2 title="Building Daily Regulation" intro="Regulation is not the absence of feelings — it is a relationship with them." quote="A regulated nervous system is built through small, repeated moments of safety." :::

Regulation grows from repetition, not intensity. This chapter offers small practices that, done consistently, teach your body a new baseline of calm.

::: box therapist title="Therapist Note"
When guiding clients through these practices, introduce one skill per session and rehearse it at least twice before adding another. Consolidation beats variety.
:::

::: box important title="Important"
If an exercise sharply increases distress, stop and ground yourself first. Regulation practices should feel challenging but tolerable.
:::
